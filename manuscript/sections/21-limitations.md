# 20. Challenges and Limitations

Figure 29 maps the limitations; every entry below is measured or explicitly recorded, none discovered during writing. **Reward engineering complexity:** R3's three terms and references (0.5 CV, 0.10 spill) are frozen constants, and the A3 study shows time-only reward agreeing with R3 on 4/4 states — but the R4 log-ratio variant could not even be fully specified, bounding how far reward redesign can currently go. **Exploration costs:** 483 of 500 SC6-counted executions (488/500 combined with the five disclosed demo TRAIN runs) were consumed to reach an UNDECIDED main comparison; the budget bought honesty (replicates, sealed evaluation) rather than victory. **Scalability constraints:** single node, `local[2]`, five synthetic families — no cluster, no streaming, no MLlib; external data appears only as one NYC-Taxi pilot (one month, one workload shape, analogical state mapping) — transfer feasibility, not a second public family (X9). **Computational overhead:** monitoring itself is cheap (6/7 PASS), but repetition studies are expensive when dependence is ignored (~3,000 wasted runs here). **Explainability limitations:** policies are inspectable yet mutually disagreeing (M8 0.20); interpretability of tables does not imply trustworthiness of any single table. **Deployment risks:** the TEST-seal and budget guard are code properties of this repository, not transferable guarantees; the AQE-on interaction rests on one mixed pilot cell (X6, pooling untested); `F3|large|s3` fails structurally on Windows; `F3_rdd|medium` is missing everywhere via the T_ref null. Finally, two verdicts fall under dependence-honest intervals and the "bimodal" label failed its own coefficient check — the paper's strong claims carry stated fragility, which is exactly why they are trustworthy as far as they go.

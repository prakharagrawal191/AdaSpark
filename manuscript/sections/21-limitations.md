# 20. Challenges and Limitations

Figure 29 maps the limitations; every entry below is measured or explicitly recorded, none discovered during writing. **Reward engineering complexity:** R3's three terms and references (0.5 CV, 0.10 spill) are frozen constants, and the A3 study shows time-only reward agreeing with R3 on 4/4 states — but the R4 log-ratio variant could not even be fully specified, bounding how far reward redesign can currently go. **Exploration costs:** 483 of 500 SC6-counted executions (488/500 combined with the five disclosed demo TRAIN runs) were consumed for a policy that the confirmatory study shows beats defaults but not static tuning; the budget bought honesty (replicates, sealed evaluation) rather than victory. **Scalability constraints:** single node, `local[2]`, five synthetic families — no cluster, no streaming, no MLlib; external data appears only as one NYC-Taxi pilot (one month, one workload shape, analogical state mapping) — transfer feasibility, not a second public family (X9). **Computational overhead:** the sampler is cheap (6/7 PASS in two studies) but the event log's cost is not pinned below 5% on four cells at n = 10–15 (EXP-014), and repetition studies are expensive when dependence is ignored (~3,000 wasted runs here). **Explainability limitations:** policies are inspectable yet mutually disagreeing on training states (M8 0.20); interpretability of tables does not imply trustworthiness of any single table. **Deployment risks:** the TEST-seal and budget guard are code properties of this repository, not transferable guarantees; AQE-on showed no benefit on six interleaved cells (EXP-013), so X6's single-cell lead is retracted (pooling untested); `F3_rdd|large` fails structurally on Windows at every probed seed; no online drift detector gates the policy; `F3_rdd|medium` is missing everywhere via the T_ref null — and on its TEST cell the learned policy's rdd_sort choice fails 5/5 where static configurations succeed. The frozen state also cannot perceive data scale: every input in the study falls in one size bin, so "unseen scale" was never visible to the agent. Finally, two verdicts fall under dependence-honest intervals and the "bimodal" label failed its own coefficient check — the paper's strong claims carry stated fragility, which is exactly why they are trustworthy as far as they go.

These limitations echo the RL literature's open problems. Reward design remains a craft with known failure modes [95]; online exploration is costly enough that offline RL has become a field of its own [97]; learned optimizers regress on inputs their training did not cover [62]; and deep policies need verification machinery that this project avoided by staying tabular [99]. Even successful ML tuners needed evaluation under production conditions before their benchmark gains were trusted [64] — the reason EXP-013 re-tests this report's own main comparison under stricter, pre-registered conditions.

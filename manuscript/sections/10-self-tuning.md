# 9. Self-Tuning Performance Optimization Layer

Self-tuning is the execution half of adaptation: partition optimization (the shuffle-partition axis of the action grid), executor configuration (the parallelism axis, via session restart), cache management for the F5 mixed pipeline's twice-consumed intermediate, and workload balancing across executors (Figure 13). The parameter lifecycle (Figure 14) runs calibrate → initialize (Q0) → adapt online → freeze → evaluate, with each transition gated: sensitivity before learning (EXP-002 gate), budget guard during learning (≤500), sealed test set during evaluation.

The ablation program tests which design choices earn their keep. A1/A2 (state variants) showed zero cross-seed greedy agreement in both representations — evidence that state design alone does not stabilize tabular policies at this sample density. The derived A3/A4 study recomputed 48 evidence pairs from the frozen EXP-002 store at zero execution cost: time-only reward agrees with R3's greedy choice on 4/4 states (differing on 8/48 pairs, all in `rdd_sort`), while the 4-action subset cannot reach the frozen B1 or two of three B2 configurations — a structural argument, measured rather than asserted, for keeping the full 12-action grid. Multi-step learning (A5, γ = 0.9) was gated NO before any build: the environment has no non-terminal path, the state has no phase field, and per-phase calibration exists nowhere — recorded with reopen conditions rather than silently dropped.

Cache management illustrates the layer's evidence-first style: rather than asserting caching benefits, the F5 pipeline's shared intermediate is timed inside the job, so any gain or loss appears in the primary metric automatically. Partition optimization carries the strongest measured result in the report (the EXP-002 gate), while executor configuration carries the subtlest — parallelism interacts with session restarts and cache lifetime, which is why the action grid varies both axes jointly instead of pretending they are independent. Workload balancing is observed through task-duration CV (an R3 term with reference 0.5) rather than controlled directly, keeping the layer's scope to parameters the grid actually sets. Together the ablations bound the design: state representation and reward shaping move little at this sample density, action granularity is load-bearing (the subset cannot reach the best static configs), and depth (multi-step) is a different system, not a larger one.

The big-data self-tuning literature frames these choices. Online DRL tuners such as DeepCAT+ adapt continuously but pay a high online exploration cost [54]; data-size-aware tuners exploit the regularity of recurring jobs [53]; GAN- and search-based tuners explore large interacting parameter spaces offline [51][50]; and parallel-program auto-tuners model interdependent parameters explicitly [52]. Database tuning adds lessons on knob selection [58][59], joint tuning of several configuration spaces [60] and the gap between benchmark and production gains [64]. Bayesian optimization [89] and multi-objective hyperparameter optimization [88] supply the non-RL comparators a tuner should face — the role AdaSpark's equal-budget random search (B4) plays in this report.

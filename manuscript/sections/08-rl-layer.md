# 7. Reinforcement Learning Optimization Layer

The RL layer is the planning organ of the MAPE-K loop. Fig. 9 traces one learning step (state, ε-greedy action from the 12-action grid, R3 reward, Q-update), and Fig. 10 shows the agent and the Spark environment exchanging actions for states and rewards under the environment-owned budget guard. Its formulation is deliberately small and frozen: a 30-state space (state-v1.5: workload class {agg, join, rdd_sort, skew_join, mixed} × size bin {S, M, L} × feedback bin {le0, gt0}), where the size bin comes from dataset manifests recorded before any run — never from live measurement — and missing history maps to the pessimistic `le0` bin by documented convention [3][4]. The 15-state context-only variant (v1) and the 2-state feedback-only variant (v2) exist for the A1/A2 ablations. Continuous telemetry (shuffle, spill, CV, CPU) is collected after execution for reward computation and is never part of the tabular state.

Actions form a 12-cell grid: shuffle partitions {16, 32, 64, 128} × execution parallelism {local-2, local-4, local-8}, with a 4-action structural subset ({0,3,6,9}) for the A4 granularity ablation. Reward is the frozen R3 formula of (1), combining normalized time improvement against a calibrated T_ref, task-balance, and spill waste, with failure scoring exactly −1:

R(s,a) = 1.0·clip((T_ref − T)/T_ref, −1, +1) + 0.2·(1 − min(1, CV/0.5)) − 0.2·min(1, spill/0.10) − 1.0·1[fail]   (1)

Here T is the measured execution time, T_ref the calibrated default-configuration median for the run's family and scale, CV the task-duration coefficient of variation, spill the spilled-to-input byte ratio, and 1[fail] is 1 for a failed or timed-out run (reward exactly −1) and 0 otherwise. Learning is ε-greedy tabular Q-learning (α = 0.2, γ = 0.0 bandit mode, ε 1.0 → 0.05 at 0.95/episode) with the standard update (2), where r is the episode's R3 reward, and the exploration schedule (3) over episode index e [3][4]:

Q(s,a) ← Q(s,a) + α·[r − Q(s,a)]   (2)

ε_e = max(0.05, 1.0·0.95^e)   (3)

Offline initialization (Q0) seeds every table entry from the median EXP-002 outcomes before the first online episode, so learning starts informed rather than blank; training then proceeds in epochs (full passes over the cell cycle) with checkpoints every 25 episodes and early stopping after 2 stable epochs. Failed episodes update the table at r = −1 rather than being skipped — the mechanism by which the agent learns to avoid pathological configurations.

Offline initialization deserves detail because it carries much of the sample efficiency. Q0 is built exclusively from authorized EXP-002 TRAIN records: per (state, action) cell, the median realized reward becomes the initial Q-value, with evidence-free pairs set optimistically to 0.5 to encourage visitation. Aggregation is median-based (the project's primary statistic), provenance records which grid observations back each entry, and the init is keyed to state-v1.5 keys — which is also why a hypothetical multi-step schema would not inherit it. Bandit mode (γ = 0.0) is the frozen default, not a simplification of convenience: with single-decision episodes the bootstrapping target never activates (0 of 232 recorded training executions ever reached it), and enabling γ = 0.9 would require a non-terminal environment path, per-phase metrics and calibrations, and a new state schema — a build, not a flag, correctly gated NO with five written reopen conditions. The early-stopping rule is documented explicitly as a budget-saving heuristic, never as convergence evidence, a distinction three project audits enforce so the stopping rule cannot be misread as a learning claim.

Three pressures from the RL literature explain the formulation. Reward design is the main lever for steering learning and a main source of unintended behaviour, which is why reward engineering and shaping now warrant their own taxonomy [95]; R3 keeps its terms few, bounded and frozen, and the A3 ablation measures how much they matter. Balancing exploration against exploitation under uncertainty remains open even for deep recurrent agents [96]; with a few hundred executions, ε-greedy with a decaying floor is the conservative choice. Sample cost dominates: offline RL learns from static datasets because online interaction is expensive or risky [97][74], and Q0 initialization from the EXP-002 grid is a small step in that direction. A table also keeps the policy inspectable — tabular agents have matched deep Q-learning's reward on a perturbed planning task while remaining explainable [98] — whereas deep policies need dedicated verification techniques [99]. Safety enters as hard guards rather than learned penalties (failure scores −1; the budget guard caps exploration), in the spirit of safe RL that constrains the explored region [100].

One property of the frozen state encoder became decisive during evaluation. Every frozen TEST input — including the "large" scale, whose datasets measure 295–386 MiB — falls below the 512 MiB boundary of size bin S, and a fresh evaluation episode uses the `le0` feedback bin. On TEST the three replicate policies therefore reach only five states (one per workload class) and act as a single per-family lookup: agg and join → `G-p8-sp16`, rdd_sort → `G-p4-sp128`, skew_join → `G-p2-sp16`, mixed → `G-p8-sp32`, identical across all three seeds. The cross-seed disagreement measured on training states (M8 = 0.20) does not appear on TEST, and the size dimension of the state never varies anywhere in the study — recorded in the Day-32 coverage audit and re-verified with zero executions before EXP-013 (DEC-053).

# 21. Future Research Directions

Figure 30 orders the agenda by evidence readiness. **Nearest (pre-designed):** the once-nearest items — the scoped AQE-on arm (EXP-005b, 35 runs, own ledger) and the validation-scoped B3 heuristic, sampler and event-log confirmations — have since been executed as X6 and X1–X3 under DEC-047/048 (X6 mixed, side-by-side only); what remains nearest is the `F3_rdd|medium` calibration backfill (gap G-05, needs its own TRAIN decision). **Medium term:** multi-agent RL over partitioned configuration subspaces (one agent per axis, coordinated through the reward), federated optimization across teams sharing Q0 calibrations without sharing data, and autonomous cluster-scale validation of the single-node findings — each requiring its own budget line and pre-registration. **Longer term** (Figure 31): LLM-assisted optimization (language models proposing configurations the RL loop adjudicates, keeping measurement as the authority), cognitive data engineering (policies that explain themselves in operator language, grounded in the traceability layer of Section 10), and dependence-aware stopping rules built from the CUSUM/ACF evidence of Section 15 — converting this report's retrospective cost finding into a prospective method. Every direction inherits the guardrails: bounded budgets, sealed evaluation, frozen analysis code, and negative results published.

Sequencing matters because budgets are finite (17 executions remain under the training cap). The validation-scoped confirmations came first — no TEST access, own register lines — and are now executed (X1 B3 7/7, X2 sampler, X3 event-log confound, under DEC-048); the F3 calibration backfill remains. The TEST-crossing work proceeded only under explicit authorization decisions — AQE-on arm X6 (DEC-047), SC7 pilot X10 (DEC-049), the F2 rematch X8 — since the seal is the project's most valuable methodological asset. External validity has one feasibility pilot (X9 Taxi, DEC-050) but its full form — a second public family, the 60-run reproducibility study — comes last because it is the most expensive and the least constrained by current gaps: it extends the claims rather than securing them. This ordering ensures that if resources run out, what remains unfinished is clearly marked future work rather than quietly weakened evidence.

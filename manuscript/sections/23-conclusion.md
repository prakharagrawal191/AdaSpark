# 22. Conclusion

AdaSpark set out to test whether Spark execution-configuration selection can be made self-adaptive with sample-efficient reinforcement learning inside a practical budget — and to report whatever the measurement said (Figure 32). What it established: configuration choice moves medians ≥10% on every tested family; a tabular learner with offline initialization trains inside 483 counted executions with zero failed submissions; monitoring overhead sits below 5% on six of seven cells; and repetition counts derived from independence assumptions fail universally because runs are regime-structured episodes, a finding with a measured ~60% waste attached. What it did not establish: superiority over static tuning (static wins), cross-seed policy agreement (0.20 vs 0.70), generalization to unseen cells, or any AQE-on interaction — each recorded as open, with the evidence that bounds it.

The durable contributions are therefore twofold: a governed, auditable control loop for Spark tuning whose every claim traces to a committed artifact, and a measurement record — including its negative results — that future work can build on without repeating. The lesson for self-adaptive systems is the report's final finding: adaptivity is not only learning faster but knowing, measurably, when not to claim learning at all.

# 22. Conclusion

AdaSpark set out to test whether Spark execution-configuration selection can be made self-adaptive with sample-efficient reinforcement learning inside a practical budget — and to report whatever the measurement said (Figure 32). What it established: configuration choice moves medians ≥10% on every tested family; a tabular learner with offline initialization trains inside 483 counted executions with zero failed submissions; and, in a pre-registered confirmatory evaluation on 42 frozen test cells, the learned policy is 4.0× faster than Spark defaults (H2 accepted) but 1.22× slower than a well-chosen static tuning (H3 rejected). The 1 Hz system sampler costs less than 5% on six of seven cells in two independent studies, the second analysed with a statistic fixed before its data existed; the Spark event log's cost could not be pinned below 5% on four cells at that study's precision. Repetition counts derived from independence assumptions fail universally because runs are regime-structured episodes, a finding with a measured ~60% waste attached.

The confirmatory studies changed the report as much as they confirmed it. The advantage over defaults held, the absence of an advantage over static tuning held, the skew-join parallelism deficit behind it held, and the sampler's low cost held. The apparent tie with random search did not — random search had converged to the static configuration, and the tie was an artifact of run order. Neither did the AQE-on lead on one join cell, a cross-day difference that vanished when the arms were interleaved, nor the size pattern in the reproducibility pilot, nor the event log's earlier pass. And a structural limitation surfaced that the descriptive record had hidden: every test input falls in one size bin, so the three replicate policies — which disagree on training states — act on test inputs as one five-rule lookup, and one of those rules fails on a test cell the agent never trained on.

The durable contributions are therefore threefold: a governed, auditable control loop for Spark tuning whose every claim traces to a recorded artifact; a measurement record, negative results included, that future work can build on without repeating; and a worked example of what pre-registered confirmation buys — an A/A control that turned a puzzling 2.2× spread into a 2% noise floor, and decision rules fixed before data that let several of the report's own claims be refuted rather than rationalized. The lessons are practical: interleave arms before comparing them, measure the noise of identical configurations before naming winners, pair conditions when measuring small overheads, and check what a policy's state can actually see before claiming it generalizes. For practitioners the immediate recommendation follows from H3: choose a static per-family configuration by interleaved measurement, and treat a learned policy as a candidate generator rather than the controller of record until its state can perceive scale. The opportunities follow directly — a state that perceives scale, multi-agent and federated tuning, and LLM-proposed configurations adjudicated by measurement (Section XXI). The lesson for self-adaptive systems is the report's final finding: adaptivity is not only learning faster but knowing, measurably, when not to claim learning at all.
